Course 03 · Case study · AI customer enablement

Explaining AI With Confidence: turning technical features into customer value.

A short Rise 360 learning experience for customer-facing teams. It helps them connect an AI feature to what a customer actually needs, be honest about what the tool can't do, and keep human review in the conversation.

Sole designer and developer
The performance problem

Customers ask about AI. Reps either oversell or overexplain.

When a customer asks whether an AI tool can be trusted, the two most common failures are opposite ones: promising more than the tool can deliver, or retreating into technical language that doesn't answer the question.

Both cost credibility. The goal was not to teach how AI works. It was to change what a rep says in the next customer conversation.

Learning objectives

What learners should be able to do

  1. Connect a specific AI feature to a customer's stated goal, in plain language.
  2. Explain a relevant limitation and what a person needs to verify.
  3. Confirm the tool's actual capability, data-use rules, and required review before promising a benefit.
Course intro

Setting the scenario

AI-presenter video produced in HeyGen · about one minute · captions included

Course structure

Three short lessons, built around practice

Lesson 01

Explain AI Value

  • Captioned scenario video
  • Spoken practice: explain a feature's value in two sentences, out loud
  • Expandable model answer to compare against
Lesson 02

Connect Features to Customer Needs

  • Sorting activity: match each AI feature to its customer value
  • Knowledge check on relying on AI summaries
Lesson 03

Respond With Confidence

  • Knowledge check on a "will it eliminate mistakes?" question
  • Select-all check: what to confirm before promising a benefit
  • Workplace takeaway for the next customer call
Key design decisions

Why it's built this way

Say it out loud before seeing the answer

WhyThe real task is spoken, in a live conversation. Asking learners to rehearse aloud first, then reveal a model response, practices retrieval and the actual output, instead of recognizing a good answer someone else wrote.

Sort features into customer value

WhyThe core skill is translation. Matching each feature (a first draft, plain-language search, a document summary) to the value a customer would feel makes that translation explicit and repeatable.

Wrong answers that mirror real mistakes

WhyThe single-answer questions each pair an honest, value-first answer with two distractors: an overpromise and a jargon answer. The distractors are the two failures from the performance problem, so choosing well is practice for the real conversation.

Responsible AI is part of the answer, not a separate module

WhyLimitations, data-use rules, and human review show up inside every model answer and every piece of feedback. Learners hear that being honest about limits is part of a credible pitch, not a disclaimer at the end.

Short, with a concrete next step

WhyCustomer-facing teams have little time for training. Three focused lessons end with one action to try in the next customer call, so the learning has a clear path to the job.

Inside the activities

A closer look

Lesson 02 · Sorting activity

Produces a first draft of a response→Gives employees a starting point they can review and revise
Searches documents using everyday language→Helps employees locate relevant information with fewer manual searches
Generates a document summary→Helps reviewers identify key points before checking the original

Lesson 03 · Knowledge check

A customer asks, "Will this AI tool eliminate mistakes in our reports?" Which response is best?

AYes. AI catches every mistake before a report is shared.Overpromise
BIf the tool supports error checking, it may flag potential issues, but your team still needs to verify accuracy.Best answer: value, limit, human review
CThe tool uses advanced machine learning to analyze reports.Jargon, doesn't answer the question
Measuring impact

How I would evaluate it in a real rollout

Learning

Scenario accuracy

Knowledge-check results by question, to find which customer questions still trip people up.

Behavior

Call observation

A short manager rubric for live or recorded calls: names the customer's goal, states a limit, names what to verify.

Results

Fewer walk-backs

Track escalations and follow-ups caused by overpromised AI capabilities, before and after rollout.

This is an independent portfolio demonstration, not work commissioned by a client. The evaluation plan above is proposed, not reported results.
